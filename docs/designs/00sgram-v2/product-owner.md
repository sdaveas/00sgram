# 00sgram v2 — product owner

Role: po. Input: `brief.md`. Output: user stories for the design stage.
Method: the persona is interviewed, not surveyed. Nothing here is code, and
nothing here touches Instagram.

## 1. Who this is for

Nikos is 58, a retired mechanic in a town outside Thessaloniki. He worked with
his hands for forty years, and still does. He has a laptop on the kitchen table and an Android phone in his jacket
pocket, and he calls the browser "the internet". He has an Instagram account
because his daughter, his son and the old football team's group post there, and
because somebody made it for him in 2019. He reads Greek; English he reads
slowly, one word at a time, and distrusts what he guesses. He knows
three computer things, used for twenty years: a window with a title
bar, a list of files with names and dates, and a media player with a play button
and a bar across the middle. He wants to see what his people posted, in order,
and be told he is finished. He does not want a conversation with an app.

## 2. Interview log

**1. PO: Where are you when you use this, and what's in front of you?**
Kitchen table, laptop, after the news. Glasses on. Phone in the yard by the
car, thumb and one finger, held away because my eyes are not twenty any more.

**2. PO: What do you actually do when you open Instagram?**
I look for Maria. Then I scroll. I don't know how far I scrolled, and then I put
the phone down unsure whether I saw everything. Half the time I saw the same
picture twice.

**3. PO: What's the first thing on that screen that bothers you?**
The row of little pictures at the bottom. Five of them, all bright. I press one
and I'm somewhere with videos of strangers, and there's no way back except the
phone's own button, which takes me out altogether. It's a shop window. I came to
see my daughter.

**4. PO: You said the app hides things. What does that mean?**
The page never ends. I keep pulling and things keep coming, some from people I
don't follow. When I come back to the top, what I was reading is gone. It's like
a mechanic who moves your tools while you work. I can't tell if I've seen
everything or if more is being kept from me.

**5. PO: The stories at the top. What happens when you touch them?**
I touch the right and it's gone before I read the caption. I touch the left and
it goes back two. Then it jumps to Kostas while I'm still on Maria's last one.
Once I sent a heart by accident and my daughter asked why, and I didn't know
what to say. Those faces have a coloured ring and I don't know whose turn it
is.

**6. PO: Is there anything from Instagram you'd keep?**
The big picture. That's it. When it's just the photo and I can see it, that's
the only good part. Everything else there is a door to somewhere else.

**7. PO: What about the words on the screen?**
Small grey words under the picture. "Suggested for you." "Sponsored." I read it
twice and still don't know what it wants. And it's in English. If a word is in
English I'm guessing, and I don't like guessing on a screen that can send
things.

**8. PO: When a video starts by itself, what do you do?**
I turn the sound down fast, or close the whole thing. It happened in the kitchen
once and my wife thought the television had turned itself on. If it starts before
I press anything, I think I did something wrong — so then I stop pressing.

**9. PO: Where do you expect the play button?**
In the middle of the film, on the picture, like a DVD player. Big triangle, and
not on my photos — a photo isn't a film.

**10. PO: Should a video make sound?**
Not until I press the little speaker. And when I press it once, remember it.
Don't make me do it again on the next one and the next. If I turned the sound
off, I meant it for the afternoon.

**11. PO: What if the video is long?**
A bar under it, and I can see how far along I am. Numbers like 1:20 / 3:40, not
a blue line I have to guess at. If I pause it and go to the door, it's still
paused when I come back. Nothing worse than returning to a man talking.

**12. PO: What should happen when there's nothing new?**
Say it. A line in Greek, "that's everything from today", and then nothing under
it. Don't leave me pulling a rope with no end — I'll keep pulling just to check,
and there goes twenty minutes.

**13. PO: And at the end of one person's stories?**
Her last picture, then a card: "That's all of Maria's. 3 of 3. Kostas has 2 if
you want." A list of names I can read, and I decide when I move. Not the app.

**14. PO: You close the laptop in the middle. Next morning?**
Same place. The first thing I hadn't seen, with a line above it: "4 new since
last time". Not the beginning again — I read that yesterday.

**15. PO: On the phone in the yard, same thing?**
Same place. It should feel like one notebook, not two. If it can't be exact on a
different machine, put me at the last thing I saw on either one and say how many
came after. Don't start me at the top and pretend.

**16. PO: What did your grandson show you that you didn't want?**
Double tap on the picture to make a heart. Swiping up for the next video.
Reels, one after another for ever. He said "it's easy, papou". Easy for fingers
that grew up with it. Mine are not small any more and they shake.

**17. PO: Which language should the buttons be in?**
Greek. "Τέλος" I understand. "End of feed" I read twice and guess, and guessing
is how I sent that heart.

**18. PO: How many people do you follow?**
About forty. The kids, cousins, the football team's group, two neighbours, the
man who fixes the boats. Some post daily, some monthly. I want who posted and
what's new, not what's popular.

**19. PO: Do you want to see how many people liked your photo?**
No. If I see three hearts I sit and think about why not four. Take the numbers
off.

## 3. First draft, rejected

Version 0, shown to Nikos the same evening. Five stories; he objected to four
before I finished reading the second.

**D1. Feed as a carousel.** *As Nikos I want the day's posts as a carousel I can
swipe through, with dots underneath, so I can move quickly between them.*

> "I don't know what a carousel is. Is it the horses? … No. I don't want to
> swipe. Swiping is for the phone and my thumb goes the wrong way and then it's
> the next thing. Put them one under the other like a list of files."

**D2. A play/pause control in the corner of every item,** so the user can see
which items are interactive.

> "Don't put a play button on a photo. I'll press it, nothing will happen, and
> I'll think it's broken, and then I'll stop trusting the whole screen. Button
> on the films only. A photo is a photo."

**D3. Story progress as a countdown ring that advances after 5 seconds,** because
that is what Instagram does.

> "That little horseshoe around the face — I see it filling and I think I'm late
> for something. And then it moves by itself before I finished reading. No. I
> press when I'm done. If I go for a glass of water, it waits."

**D4. A red badge on the tab with the number of new items.**

> "A red circle with a number is a bill I haven't paid. And I can't clear it, so
> it's a bill I can never pay. Take it off. If there's new things, write it in
> the status line like a normal program."

**D5. A "Discover" row of accounts he might like.** He did not object; he asked
whether he had to look at it, and said "leave it out anyway, the list is long
enough".

### What changed between drafts

| Draft | Final | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Carousel with dots | Vertical list, newest first | Swipe is a gesture he gets wrong; a list of files he knows. |
| Play control on every item | Play control only on video | A control that does nothing costs trust in all of them. |
| Countdown ring, 5 s auto-advance | Static "2 από 5", press to advance | A filling ring reads as a deadline; auto-advance takes the decision away. |
| Red badge with a count | Status-line text, no badge | An unclearable number is a debt; the brief forbids a red dot. |
| "Discover" row | Cut (non-goal) | He has no use for people he does not follow. |
| (absent) | End card with count and date | He asked twice; the draft had no end story at all. |
| (absent) | Resume in place, either machine | He assumes it exists; the draft would fail him on the second morning. |

## 4. Final user stories

Nine stories. `[HARD]` marks a brief constraint engineering may not weaken.

### S1. Order, and one thing at a time
As Nikos I want my people's posts in the order they posted them, one under the
other, so that I can read down the page like a list and know nothing is being
reordered behind my back.
- API order and rendered DOM order are identical; a test compares the visible
  author/timestamp sequence to the `/api/posts` sequence.
- No horizontal scroll container and no side-by-side cards at 390x844,
  1280x800, 844x390.
- Author and local timestamp above each picture ("Μαρία · χθες 21:14"); nothing
  from an unfollowed account is inserted between posts.

### S2. A picture big enough to see
As Nikos I want the picture as wide as the window with nothing on top of it, so
that I can see my granddaughter's face without pressing anything.
- Never cropped, never smaller than container width; served at ≥ container width
  in CSS pixels.
- Over the picture: nothing. No gradient, corner icons, text or badges. One
  control under it, "Πιο μεγάλο", opens a full-window view; Esc or a close box
  returns to the same scroll position.
- Caption below, ≥16px, contrast ≥ 4.5:1; Greek text left as Greek, names not
  translated.

### S3. A film that waits
As Nikos I want a film to sit still with a play button until I press it, so that
nothing starts talking in the kitchen without me.
- After load every `<video>` has `paused === true` and no autoplay, asserted
  after a 3 s wait per feed page.
- One play control, centred on the frame, ≥44x44, label "Αναπαραγωγή". Count of
  play controls equals count of video items; images have none.
- Controls: ◀ 10 s, pause, 10 s ▶, scrub bar with `mm:ss / mm:ss`. A pause
  survives leaving the view and coming back.
- Sound off on first load; mute state read from and written to `/api/settings`,
  surviving segment change and reload. `[HARD — mute remembered]`

### S4. The end says it's the end
As Nikos I want a line that says that's everything, so that I can close the
laptop without wondering whether something is still coming.
- After the last item a terminal block reads "Αυτά είναι όλα. 12 αναρτήσεις, από
  τις 9 Νοεμβρίου", with the count shown and the oldest date.
- It is terminal: no request after it, scrolling past fetches nothing, reloading
  at the end shows the same block. `[HARD — finite feed, explicit end state]`
- When nothing is new, the end block is what opens, not an empty page: "Τίποτα
  καινούργιο από την Τρίτη."
- A "Πάνω" control at the end returns to the first post without re-fetching.

### S5. Come back to where I was
As Nikos I want to open it tomorrow and be where I stopped, so that I don't read
the same three pictures again.
- Position is stored server-side and survives browser close, container restart,
  and laptop-to-phone switch.
- On open the view starts at the first unseen post with a line above it: "4 νέα
  από την τελευταία φορά." If nothing is unseen it starts at the end block.
- Re-sync appends; it never reorders or re-marks a seen post, and a post seen on
  one device is not unseen on the other.
- Position never depends on localStorage or a cookie alone.

### S6. Stories: one person at a time
As Nikos I want each person's stories one after another with their name and
which one I'm on, so that I always know who I'm looking at and how long it is.
- Header shows display name and "2 από 5". `[HARD — person, segment counter]`
- Segments advance only on an explicit press; the last segment of an account
  leads to that account's end card, never to the next account's first segment.
  `[HARD — independent per-account progress]`
- A list of accounts with stories is one press away from anywhere, showing name
  and unseen count as text. Choosing one opens that account's first unseen
  segment and touches no other account's position. `[HARD — jump to any other
  person with stories]`
- Back from an account's first segment returns to the list, not the previous
  account.

### S7. Marking seen is my job
As Nikos I want a button that marks a story as seen, so that nothing moves
before I have finished looking at it.
- No timer advances, marks or hides anything: a 60 s idle test leaves the
  segment and its seen state unchanged.
- "Το είδα" marks the current segment seen, then shows the next segment of the
  same account or that account's end card. `[HARD — markable as seen without a
  countdown deciding it]`
- Marking is idempotent; a double press does not skip two segments.
- Seen/unseen shows in the account list as text ("είδες 2 από 5") plus a grey or
  green marker — never a red dot, never a filling ring.

### S8. The things stuck on a story stay readable
As Nikos I want to read what is attached to a story without it disappearing, so
that I know which place that is and which song that is.
- Each sticker renders as a row below the frame: type, label, target as text
  (handle, place name, URL). No sticker opens anything on its own.
- Nothing navigates away while reading; opening a link is a separate control,
  and the picture stays on screen with the list shown. `[HARD — stickers
  readable without leaving the picture]`
- Rows ≥16px, selectable, never truncated without an expand control.

### S9. Never asked for anything
As Nikos I want no buttons that ask me to like, follow, share, reply or buy, so
that I cannot send a heart by accident.
- No like, comment, share, save, follow, unfollow, reply or reaction control on
  any view; a test enumerates each route's DOM and asserts the set is empty.
- No code path issues a mutating Instagram request; the only story write is the
  read that registers a view, already accepted by the owner. `[HARD — no writes
  to Instagram, ever]`
- No modal, banner or tooltip asks him to do something. Only two unprompted
  messages may appear — "no connection", "sync failed, last good copy shown" —
  both dismissible and shown once per session.
- No engagement number anywhere: no likes, views, followers, "seen by".

## 5. Non-goals

1. Direct messages, story replies, comment threads.
2. Explore, search, discovery, "suggested for you", anyone he does not follow.
3. Every write to Instagram: post, like, comment, follow, share, save, react.
   Not "off by default" — absent.
4. Reels, continuous video, swipe-up, autoplay of any kind.
5. Engagement counts of any sort: likes, views, followers, "seen by".
6. Notifications, badges, unread dots, sounds, anything that nags.
7. Creating or uploading: posts, stories, camera, filters.
8. Ranking, "top posts", personalisation. Order is chronological, always.
9. Multiple accounts, profile switching, analytics for his own account.
10. Settings screens with options. Decided defaults, no switches to get wrong.
11. Live or broadcast video; scheduled or "close friends" content. No themes.

## 6. "Like it's 2000", in his words

Ask him what a program should look like and he answers with things:

1. **A title bar.** "00sgram — τα νέα" across the top, with minimise and close
   boxes where they have always been. The window is the program; the close box
   means close.
2. **A list of files.** Name, date, and what is inside. His words: "like when
   you open the folder and it says Μαρία, 14 Νοεμβρίου, three files". A list
   ends. A list has a bottom edge.
3. **A media player transport.** Play, pause, a bar across the middle, and
   `1:20 / 3:40` written out. "The bar tells me how long the film is. It doesn't
   tell me to hurry."
4. **A progress number that is honest and slow.** "12% done" for loading, "3 από
   12" for where he is. Numbers that stay still are trustworthy; numbers that
   count down are a deadline. Hence: a static "2 από 5" is fine, a filling ring
   is not.
5. **A dial-up status line.** A strip along the bottom: "Συνδέομαι…" then
   "Συνδέθηκα — 6 νέα". A program that says what it is doing, in words, then
   stops talking.
6. **A file menu** — Αρχείο, Προβολή, Βοήθεια — with three or four items each
   and nothing hidden behind an icon he has to decode. Plus "Σελίδα 1 από 3" at
   the bottom of a list: a page number promises a fixed number of pages.

What they have in common, said plainly: **the screen does not change unless he
changes it, every control says what it does in Greek, and every screen has an
edge he can reach.** That is the design brief — a program from 2000 that happens
to know about his daughter's photographs.

## 7. What would make him close the laptop

Worst first. Each one is enough on its own.

1. **Something moves or makes a sound before he pressed anything.** Video
   starting, a story advancing, a page loading under his finger. He concludes he
   did something wrong, and stops pressing.
2. **A press does nothing, or does something other than what it says.** A play
   button on a photo, a control needing a double press, a link going nowhere. He
   cannot tell whether it is the app or himself, and stops trusting it.
3. **He cannot get back.** Browser back throws him to a blank page, "home" takes
   him to the top, the story viewer has no way to the list. Twice and he uses
   the phone's gallery instead.
4. **Words he has to guess.** English labels, icons without text, grey 11px
   captions. Guessing is how he sent a heart.
5. **No end.** The list keeps growing, the count never settles, no "that's
   everything". He scrolls to check, finds nothing, closes the laptop feeling
   behind.

## Decisions

Taken here because the brief is silent; all reversible except the last item.

- **UI language is Greek**, no language switcher. He reads Greek; English labels
  are a guessing game. (Reversible: one strings table.)
- **Sound off by default**, choice remembered. Mute is the safe default in a
  kitchen with another person in it.
- **"Seen" is manual only.** A static counter is allowed, a countdown is not.
- **One appearance, no theme settings.** A settings page is one more screen to
  get lost on.
- **Not reversible, by the brief:** no writes to Instagram, and the v1 contract
  items marked `[HARD]`. Dropping one comes back to the owner, not to this doc.
